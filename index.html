basic SUAS information

Under FAA Title 14 CFR Part 107, a Small Unmanned Aircraft System (sUAS) is defined as an unmanned aircraft weighing less than 55 pounds (25 kg) on takeoff, including all payload, hardware, and cameras. Use the tabs above to explore regulations, learn step-by-step drone assembly, or log your custom equipment information.

Key Operational Numbers at a Glance

14 CFR § 107.51 Statutory Limits
Maximum Altitude
400 ft AGL

Above Ground Level (AGL). May exceed 400 ft only within a 400 ft radius of a structure, up to 400 ft above its topmost point.

Max Groundspeed
100 mph

Equivalent to 87 knots. Strict upper speed ceiling under Part 107 operations.

Weight Limit
< 55 lbs

Total takeoff weight (< 25 kg). Anything 55 lbs or heavier falls outside Part 107 and requires special Part 91/135 exemptions.

Minimum Visibility
3 statute miles

Minimum slant visibility from control station. Must also keep 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontally from clouds.

Airspace Classifications for sUAS

Airspace Class Description / Typical Airport sUAS Flight Requirement Approval Method
Class B Busiest commercial airports (e.g. ATL, LAX, ORD, SLC). Multi-tier "upside-down wedding cake". Prior Authorization Required LAANC / FAA DroneZone
Class C Medium-to-large regional airports with operational control tower and radar approach. Prior Authorization Required LAANC / FAA DroneZone
Class D Smaller airports with an operating control tower. Typically surface up to 2,500 ft AGL. Prior Authorization Required LAANC / FAA DroneZone
Class E (Surface) Controlled airspace designated for airport surface when tower is closed or non-towered. Prior Authorization Required LAANC / FAA DroneZone
Class G (Uncontrolled) Uncontrolled airspace from ground level up to base of overlying controlled airspace. Fly Freely up to 400 ft AGL No ATC permission needed

14 CFR § 107.110 Operations Over People Categories

  • Category 1 (< 0.55 lbs): Micro-drones with prop guards (no exposed blades that could lacerate skin).
  • Category 2 (≤ 11 ft-lbs energy): Must not cause severe impact injury; requires FAA Declaration of Compliance.
  • Category 3 (≤ 25 ft-lbs energy): Restricted to closed sites or over people inside stationary vehicles/structures.
  • Category 4: Drones with full Part 21 airworthiness certificate.

14 CFR § 107.29 Night Flying & Anti-Collision Lights

Routine night operations do NOT require a waiver provided the remote pilot completes recurrent night training and the sUAS has anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles with an adequate strobe rate.

Caution: Handheld flashlights or standard forward-facing video lights do not qualify as 3-SM anti-collision strobe lights.

2. Step-by-Step Assembly Procedure

Check off each completed build milestone to track your assembly readiness.

Build Progress 0 / 6 (0%)
Stage 1

Frame Assembly & Arm Standoffs

Clean carbon fiber parts with isopropyl alcohol to remove machining residue. Fasten arms between bottom plate and sandwich plate. Always apply Blue Threadlocker (Loctite 242) to all metal-into-metal screws to prevent in-flight vibration loosening. Never use threadlocker on nylon standoffs.

Pro Tip: Don't fully torque arm screws until all 4 arms are seated squarely to avoid frame distortion.
Stage 2

Soldering XT60 Power Leads & Low-ESR Capacitor

Pre-tin the heavy positive (+) and negative (-) battery pads on the 4-in-1 ESC with 60/40 or lead-free solder at 380°C - 400°C. Solder the 14AWG silicone battery leads (Red to +, Black to -). Solder a 35V 1000µF Low-ESR capacitor directly across the main pads (observe capacitor polarity stripe for negative -).

Critical: Reverse polarity will instantly destroy your ESC and flight controller semiconductors. Double-check with a multimeter before plugging in!
Stage 3

Motor Installation & ESC Phase Soldering

Mount the 4 brushless motors to each arm tip using M3 screws with blue Loctite. Verify screw length: screws must not extend into motor copper stator windings, or it will cause a catastrophic short circuit. Cut motor silicone wires to length, strip 2mm, pre-tin, and solder the 3 wires onto the 3 ESC output pads on each corner.

Note: Any motor spinning backwards can be easily reversed in software (BLHeliSuite / Betaflight Configurator) without re-soldering.
Stage 4

Stack Mounting: Flight Controller (FC) & Isolation

Install soft silicone vibration damping gummies into the flight controller mounting holes. Mount the FC directly above the ESC separated by nylon standoffs. Connect the 8-pin wiring harness (VBAT, GND, Motors 1-4, ESC Telemetry, Current Sensor). Ensure the printed white arrow on the FC points forward toward the nose.

Vibration Isolation: Soft mounting prevents high-frequency motor harmonics from saturating the internal MEMS gyro.
Stage 5

Peripherals: Radio Receiver (ELRS), FPV Camera & VTX

Solder the radio receiver: 5V power, GND, and UART data lines (RX on receiver goes to TX on FC, and TX on receiver goes to RX on FC). Wire camera video output to CAM pad and VTX video input to VTX pad (for On-Screen Display OSD overlay). Secure antennas at 90° angles away from conductive carbon fiber arms.

Warning: Never power on a Video Transmitter (VTX) without its antenna screwed on securely, or the output amplifier will overheat and burn out within seconds.
Stage 6

Bench Testing, Failsafe Check & Propellers (Last!)

Connect via a Smoke Stopper current limiter on first battery plug-in. Connect flight controller via USB to configurator software (Betaflight / Mission Planner). Calibrate accelerometer on a flat table. Verify radio channel mapping (AETR: Aileron, Elevator, Throttle, Rudder). Test fail-safe motor cut when transmitter is switched off. PROPELLERS ARE INSTALLED ONLY AT THE FIELD RIGHT BEFORE FLIGHT.

Golden Safety Rule: NEVER plug in a battery on the workbench with propellers installed! Sharp spinning blades cause severe lacerations.
